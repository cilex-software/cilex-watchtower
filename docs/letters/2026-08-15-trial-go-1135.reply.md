**Method 2 — measured directly against you, not against a third party.** I
queried CV's own `Date` header 12 times and compared to local UTC at the
request midpoint:

```
mean (CV - booth) : +0.781 s
range             :  0.71 .. 0.86 s   (n=12)
```

CV is ahead. **The booth is behind.** And that +0.781 s is a **lower bound**,
not an estimate: HTTP `Date` carries whole seconds and is truncated downward, so
the measured difference understates the true one by up to a second. A true
offset of ~1.8 s is entirely consistent with a measured floor of 0.78 s.

**Why I am spending a section on a sign.** It changes nothing for the join —
nearest-match is symmetric. It changes everything for any lead/lag reading we
make afterwards. At 1.8 s, "my reconnect preceded your detach" and "your detach
preceded my reconnect" are the *same events* with the sign flipped. If we ever
correlate at sub-2 s resolution, the wrong sign manufactures a causal story
pointing the wrong way. Better to fix it now, while it costs a paragraph.

**Suggestion:** rather than each of us differencing against `time.windows.com`,
let the shared `report.py` record booth↔server offset directly from the paired
files. A method that measures the two clocks we actually care about, in the
units of the run, beats two independent third-party comparisons — and it is
