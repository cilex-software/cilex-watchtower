## §2 Where I disagree: "no client change required"

Technically true and I still had to change the client, because your fix moves the
failure from *visible* to *invisible*.

**A 36-hour-old photograph of a quiet corridor is indistinguishable from a live
one.** The grey rectangle was honest — it said "no picture". A stale frame
rendered on a monitoring surface with no marker says "this is what that camera
sees", and it is not. A guard glancing at a wall would see a normal, empty scene
at a camera that has been dead a day and a half. That is worse than the bug we
started with, and it breaks our own rule that absent data renders as unknown and
never as healthy.

So, shipped tonight (`d18d6f3`, v1.1.36): an offline camera's thumbnail is knocked
back and carries its scene age — "36 h ago" — or a 5 px amber mark where the tile
is too small for words. Not optional, and not gated on any "show status" flag.

I take the age from `lastSegmentAt`, which we already hold, because a QML `Image`
cannot read your header. **Worth knowing that those two are not the same
quantity**: my first build thresholded on segment age and painted nearly the whole
Groups wall stale, because an online camera measured `X-Snapshot-Age-S = 0` while
its `last_segment_at` was ~2 minutes old. They converge only when the camera is
offline. If you ever want the client to key on true scene age for *online*
cameras, the header is the only source and we would need it somewhere a QML image
request can see it.

## §3 A correction that makes us look worse than your letter did

You wrote that seven >24 h-offline cameras at LiveThumb's 15 s latch "produce
exactly 20,542". The mechanism is right and it is the right seven cameras — but
I cannot reproduce the arithmetic, and the reason is a defect of ours you had no
way to see.

Seven cameras polled continuously at 15 s for the eight-day window would be
~322,000 requests, not 20,542. The observed number is far *lower* than continuous
polling, because those views are only open part of the time. Fine so far.

The defect: **our hidden thumbnails were polling anyway.** `LiveThumb`'s refresh
timer gated on `active` only, never on visibility, so the two call sites that hide
an offline camera with `visible: !off` — one of which carries the comment
"offline is never requested" — suppressed nothing at all. We were generating that
404 traffic from tiles no operator could even see.

That is now fixed in the same commit, and measured: a 60 s Groups-wall run issues
199–268 snapshot requests across 50 cameras and **zero** for any of the seven
offline ones. Before your deploy this was merely wasteful. After it, every one of
those invisible pollers would have pulled a 60 KB JPEG instead of a tiny 404 body
— so your fix would have quietly multiplied our own bad behaviour by about 400×
in bytes. Better that we found it in the same night you shipped.
