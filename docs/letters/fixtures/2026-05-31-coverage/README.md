# Footage-coverage fixtures (A7 + E4) — 2026-05-31

Generated by `tools/fixtures/gen_coverage_fixtures.py` (cilex-vision) by driving
the **real** `GET /review/coverage` endpoint (`routers/coverage.py`) over a fake
DB pool — so field names, the row→dict transform, and the response assembly are
byte-faithful to the live endpoint. No live DB / deployed stack needed.

## Files
- `coverage-single.response.json` — `?camera_id=…&tz=Europe/Moscow` (A7). Drives
  `PlaybackClient::fetchCoverage`.
- `coverage-batch-union.response.json` — `?camera_ids=A,B&tz=Europe/Moscow` (E4).
  Drives `fetchCoverageUnion`. Two cameras with different day sets; `union` = ['2026-05-28', '2026-05-29', '2026-05-30', '2026-05-31'].

## What to verify
- **Single:** `retention_days` (int, here 35) + `days[]`, each
  `{date, has_footage, seconds, coverage_ratio, tiers, first, last}`. Your
  `fetchCoverage` reads `retention_days` + `days[].{date, has_footage}`; the
  extra fields are additive — confirm you ignore them.
- **Batch:** `retention_days` + `union` (sorted `YYYY-MM-DD` of days ANY camera
  has footage) + `per_camera` (dense per-camera `days[]`; you may ignore it).
- **`days` is SPARSE** — only days WITH footage appear, and `has_footage` is always
  `true` on returned entries. Absent days = no footage (render unmarked). Matches
  your "sparse list of footage-days is sufficient".
- **tz** is echoed (`"Europe/Moscow"`); `first`/`last` are in that tz (`+03:00`).
  The real day-bucketing is `date(start_time AT TIME ZONE tz)` in SQL — send your
  operator's IANA tz on the request or days bucket in UTC.

## Caveats
- The day set + timestamps are illustrative (synthetic rows); the endpoint's
  shape/transform/assembly are real. `seconds`/`coverage_ratio` use
  `max(sub, main)` per day (never summed across streams). `generated_at` is the
  generation time.
