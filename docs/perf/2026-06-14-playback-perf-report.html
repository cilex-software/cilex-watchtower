Findings & fixes
  • ✔ Fixed: jumping to a motion event whose moment is past the camera's last recorded frame (common on motion-triggered cams like .106) used to freeze the player and re-fetch the final segment forever. Now it lands on the nearest recorded footage, or shows an honest “No footage was recorded around this moment” when footage is >90 s away. (5 events on .106 were affected — they reference motion the camera detected but never recorded.)
  • ✔ Fixed: any seek to the exact end-of-footage could strand the pipeline at EOS — seeks now keep a 3 s playable headroom.
  • ✔ Hardened: a deferred event-seek can no longer resolve against a previous session's coverage during a session swap (armed-on-ready gate + fragments cleared on open).
  • ◆ Note (backend/recording): .106 has motion events with no corresponding footage (e.g. 06-11 23:06–23:17, recording stopped 23:05:18). That's a recording-coverage gap on a motion-triggered camera, not a client perf issue — the client now reports it honestly.

Method & caveats
  • Each row = pause → seek → play, timed until the playhead actually advances again (the “time from FF/RW to playback starting”).
  • In-session = within one 24 h review session; ±1 day / ±5 days necessarily open a new session (sessions cap at 24 h).
  • Wall in-session figures benefit from the backend render-ahead/cache on an already-touched day; a truly cold far-jump on the wall is the “day jump” row (~4–5 s).
  • Backward seeks run ~1.5–2× slower than forward (decoder flush + re-fetch) — expected, still well within norm.
  • One first-event boot artifact (evt-1) excluded; it reflects a launch-sequence race, not single-click behaviour.
